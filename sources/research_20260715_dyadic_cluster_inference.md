# Research note: graph-aware inference for shared-image dyadic pairs

This note records the public statistical-method sources consulted on 15 July 2026 before proposing the PF-ERI v2 graph-aware interval. It contains no PF-ERI outcome data.

Aronow, Samii, and Assenova developed a nonparametric sandwich variance estimator specifically for dyadic observations whose errors may be correlated whenever two dyads share a member. Their formulation treats the number of underlying units, rather than the nominal number of dyads, as the key asymptotic dimension. The paper also extends the method to weighted and repeated observations. Those properties match PF-ERI, where each canonical pair is a dyad, each image is a member, and deployment-queue estimates may require inverse-inclusion weighting. The article is “Cluster-Robust Variance Estimation for Dyadic Data,” *Political Analysis*, 23(4), 564–577, DOI [10.1093/pan/mpv018](https://doi.org/10.1093/pan/mpv018). The accessible preprint is available at [arXiv:1312.3398](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.3398).

Cameron, Gelbach, and Miller developed the more general inclusion–exclusion sandwich construction for non-nested multiway clustering. Their method is relevant background, but ordinary two-way clustering is not by itself an adequate description of PF-ERI because canonical endpoint ordering is arbitrary and one image can appear on either side of different pairs. The dyadic estimator is therefore the more direct primary method. The published article is “Robust Inference with Multiway Clustering,” *Journal of Business & Economic Statistics*, 29(2), 238–249, DOI [10.1198/jbes.2010.07136](https://doi.org/10.1198/jbes.2010.07136); an earlier open working-paper record is available from the [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/t0327).

MacKinnon, Nielsen, and Webb review modern cluster-robust practice and emphasize that the correct clustering structure must follow the dependence-generating process rather than convenience. Their review also discusses cluster jackknife and wild-cluster bootstrap methods as finite-sample tools. For PF-ERI, those methods remain possible sensitivity analyses, but they should not replace the dyadic estimator without a separate validation showing that their resampling unit preserves the symmetric shared-image graph. The review is “Cluster-robust inference: A guide to empirical practice,” *Journal of Econometrics*, 232(2), 272–299, DOI [10.1016/j.jeconom.2022.04.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2022.04.001).

The source review supports a dyadic cluster-robust primary interval and rejects an ordinary independent-row interval. It does not establish that image sharing is the only possible dependence. Camera, site, identity, or higher-order network dependence could remain after image-node clustering, so those structures require pre-specified sensitivity analyses when their restricted metadata are available.
